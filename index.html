How I work

I like problems that sit between fields

Chemistry taught me to reason carefully about what is actually happening. Hardware taught me that a clever idea still has to survive contact with a breadboard. Machine learning is where I spend the most time, usually cleaning messy data until a model has something real to learn from.

What ties it together is that I would rather get one thing working end to end than get any single piece perfect. Most of what I have built started as a question I could not stop poking at.

What I've built
Independent researchPyTorch

BloomCast

A model that forecasts harmful algae blooms in New Jersey lakes. I pulled together a few different kinds of data, satellite imagery, water measurements, and weather, and trained a multimodal deep-learning model in PyTorch to predict where blooms are likely to show up. It is the project I have put the most of myself into.

RoboticsColumbia SHAPE

Quadruped walking robot in progress

A four-legged robot I'm building through Columbia's SHAPE program. Right now I am working through the walking gait and balance, and learning how much of robotics is getting the hardware and the code to agree with each other.

Research · MITKellis Group

Data-viz tool at the Kellis Lab

A summer working on a data-visualization tool, including the controls that size each bubble by its value, and a small agentic workflow I built to automate parts of the analysis. It was my first time writing code that other researchers would actually use.
